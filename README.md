# 🚩 Bootcamp & IT School Red Flags
### How to Spot Ineffective Training Providers Before You Sign Any Contract

Every year, thousands of motivated career changers waste months of their life and thousands of dollars (or government training vouchers) on IT schools that fail to deliver job-ready skills.

Based on **30+ years of experience in the IT industry**, this guide helps you look behind polished marketing promises and protect yourself from bad training programs.

---

## ⚠️ The Core Problem: "Paid Per Head, Not For Your Success"
Many coding bootcamps, private academies, and NGO-style training programs operate on a broken business model:
* **Government-Funded / Voucher Programs:** They get paid by the state as soon as you sign the enrollment contract – regardless of whether you ever get hired.
* **High-Ticket Commercial Bootcamps:** They spend massive budgets on aggressive social media ads promising *"Become a Developer in 12 Weeks"*, using high-pressure sales tactics to lock you into expensive loans or Income Share Agreements (ISAs).

Once they have your signature (or your voucher), the quality of teaching often drops dramatically.

---

## 🚨 10 Major Red Flags to Watch Out For

1. **"No Teachers, Just Peer-to-Peer Learning" (Self-Study Disguised as a School)**
   * *The pitch:* "We don't have teachers because in real IT you have to figure everything out yourself!"
   * *The reality:* While self-learning is important in IT, paying thousands of dollars (or wasting a training voucher) just to sit in a room and Google things with other beginners is not education – it is cost-cutting by the school. Beginners need experienced mentors to prevent bad habits and explain *why* things work.

2. **Everyone Gets Pushed Into "Full-Stack Web Development"**
   * *The red flag:* The school offers no real career orientation and puts every student into the exact same JavaScript/React coding track.
   * *The reality:* Junior developer jobs are the hardest entry point in IT right now. Good programs assess your strengths first and show you realistic paths like **IT Support, QA Testing, Data Analytics, or Cloud Operations**.

3. **Unrealistic Timelines ("Job-Ready in 8 to 12 Weeks")**
   * *The reality:* Nobody becomes a job-ready software engineer from scratch in 12 weeks. Cramming 6 months of material into 8 weeks only creates burnout and superficial copy-paste knowledge.

4. **No Transparent Curriculum Before Signing**
   * *The red flag:* They refuse to show you a detailed week-by-week syllabus or sample lessons before you enroll.

5. **Outdated Tech Stacks or Random Buzzwords**
   * *The red flag:* The curriculum looks like a random soup of 15 buzzwords (Blockchain, AI, Web3, Java, Python, React, Docker – all in 3 months) instead of deep, solid fundamentals in one clear job profile.

6. **Fake or Manipulated "95% Hiring Rate" Claims**
   * *The reality:* Look closely at the fine print. Many schools count unpaid internships, part-time non-IT jobs, or hiring their own graduates as "teaching assistants" into their success statistics.

7. **Instructors Without Real-World Industry Experience**
   * *The red flag:* Your "senior instructor" graduated from the exact same bootcamp 3 months ago and has never worked on a real production system in a company.

8. **High-Pressure Sales Calls ("Only 2 Seats Left!")**
   * *The reality:* Serious educational institutions give you time to think, compare alternatives, and talk to alumni. Artificial urgency is a classic sales trap.

9. **Reinventing the Wheel Instead of Using Industry Standards**
   * *The red flag:* They lock you into proprietary learning platforms instead of teaching you with real-world tools (GitHub, Linux, Microsoft Learn, Playwright, official vendor docs) that you will actually use on the job.

10. **Zero Career Support Beyond a Generic CV Template**
    * *The reality:* Sending you a PDF on "how to fix your LinkedIn" is not career coaching.

---

## ✅ 7 Questions You MUST Ask Before Signing Anything

Ask the admissions advisor these exact questions – and watch how they react:

- [ ] **"Who are the actual instructors, and how many years of real-world IT industry experience do they have?"**
- [ ] **"Can I speak directly to 2 or 3 graduates who finished this exact course 6 months ago (not selected ambassadors)?"**
- [ ] **"What percentage of graduates are working in a full-time, paid IT role 6 months after graduation – and how do you define that number?"**
- [ ] **"How much live instruction and 1-on-1 code/work review by senior professionals is included per week?"**
- [ ] **"Do you offer career orientation *before* enrollment to check if another IT role (like QA or IT Support) fits my background better?"**
- [ ] **"What happens if I realize after 2 weeks that the pace or role does not fit me? Can I cancel without financial penalty?"**
- [ ] **"Why should I pay for this course when official vendor paths (like Microsoft Learn or ISTQB) are available for free?"**

## 🟢 Green Flags: What a Great, Honest IT Program Looks Like

Knowing what to avoid is only half the battle. Whether you are evaluating a free vendor path, a paid course, or a live workshop, a **serious and high-quality program** will always be transparent about these 8 things:

### 1. Full Curriculum & Skill Transparency
* **Clear breakdown:** You see *before* enrolling which exact skills and tools you will learn in Week 1, Week 4, and Week 8.
* **No buzzword bingo:** Instead of promising "everything in 3 months," the program focuses on solid fundamentals for **one specific job profile**.

### 2. Honest Prerequisites (Who Is This Actually For?)
* **Clear starting line:** The provider clearly states what you need before starting (e.g., basic computer literacy, English reading level, logical thinking, or specific hardware).
* **Pre-course orientation:** Good educators help you check if the role fits your personality *before* you commit.

### 3. Transparent Teaching & Learning Methodology
* **How you actually learn:** You know upfront how the course is structured – live interactive sessions, hands-on labs, real code/work reviews, or guided self-study.
* **Real mentors, not just videos:** Experienced practitioners are available to answer "why" something works, unblock you when you are stuck, and review your results.

### 4. A Concrete, Verifiable Skill Portfolio at the End
* **Proof over promises:** At the end of the program, you don't just walk away with a PDF participation certificate. You have **tangible projects** (e.g., a GitHub repository, documented bug reports, a working home lab, or automated test scripts) that you can show in a job interview.

### 5. Realistic Target Job Titles
* **Clear job mapping:** The program explicitly names the exact entry-level job titles you can apply for with this portfolio (e.g., *Junior QA Tester, Tier 1 IT Support Specialist, Junior Data Analyst*) – without pretending you will start as a Senior Architect.

### 6. Industry-Recognized Exams & Certificates (No "Fantasy" Diplomas)
* **Honest value of certificates:** Good providers tell you the truth: internal school certificates carry little weight on their own. Instead, they prepare you for **globally recognized vendor or industry certifications** (e.g., *Microsoft Fundamentals, ISTQB Foundation Level, Linux Essentials*) and explain how those exams work.

### 7. Clear Long-Term Career Progression ("What Comes Next?")
* **Beyond the first job:** A good roadmap shows you how your entry-level role serves as a stepping stone – for example, how **IT Support** leads into **SysAdmin or Cloud**, or how **Manual QA** grows into **Test Automation**.

### 8. Fair Conditions & Zero Pressure
* **Time to decide:** You get a clear syllabus, transparent pricing (or free access), and fair cancellation terms without high-pressure sales tactics.

## 📋 Quick Comparison: Bad School vs. Good Program

| Criteria | 🚩 Bad Bootcamp / School | 🟢 Good Program / Workshop |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Curriculum** | Vague promises, hidden until you sign | Public, step-by-step skill breakdown |
| **Teaching** | "Figure it out yourself" / inexperienced tutors | Structured guidance + experienced practitioners |
| **Outcome** | Generic attendance certificate | Concrete GitHub portfolio + industry exam prep |
| **Job Promise** | "Everyone becomes a Full-Stack Dev!" | Realistic entry roles + long-term growth path |
